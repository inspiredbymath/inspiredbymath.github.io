The Monty Hall Problem

Three doors — one hides a car, two hide goats. Pick a door; the host opens another to reveal a goat. Should you switch, or stay? Play a few rounds and watch the statistics settle.

Why switching works

When you first pick, you have a 1/3 chance of choosing the car and a 2/3 chance of choosing a goat. The host always opens a goat door from the rest. If you picked a goat initially (2/3 of the time), switching always wins. If you picked the car (1/3 of the time), switching loses. So switching wins two-thirds of the time — twice as often as staying.
